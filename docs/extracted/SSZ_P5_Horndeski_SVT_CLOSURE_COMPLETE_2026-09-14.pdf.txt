Abstract
We investigate whether a prescribed regular, static, spherically symmetric strong-field geometry
arising within the Segmented Spacetime (SSZ) program can be realized as a dynamically admissible
solution of a covariant scalar–vector–tensor theory rather than merely as an effective metric ansatz.
The geometry studied here is the frozen P5 background, described by temporal and radial metric
functions f(r) and h(r), together with a monotonic scalar profile \phi(r)\equiv\Xi(r). Its
strong-field region contains two circular null orbits: an outer unstable light ring at

                                                    𝑟
                                               𝑥≡      = 1.5,
                                                    𝑟𝑠

and an inner stable light ring at


                                           𝑥s ≃ 1.41616064.

The outer orbit reproduces the general-relativistic Schwarzschild shadow scale and the corresponding
eikonal Lyapunov scale, while the inner orbit forms a shallow stable null potential well with a relative
depth of approximately 4.18\times10^{-3}.
The frozen geometry is not treated here as an arbitrary numerical triplet. Its current source-locked
closure can be written in terms of a primary susceptibility \chi=d\Xi/du=(1-\Xi)^{3/2}\mathcal
P_5(\Xi), with f=(1+\Xi)^{-2} and a radial closure generated by the ratio \chi/\chi_{\rm GR}.
Independent regression reproduces the frozen temporal and radial metric profiles at machine preci-
sion. The same closure is horizonless (f>0, h>0), regular at the center, and reproduces Schwarzschild
through cubic order in the weak-field compactness expansion. At the outer light ring it additionally
matches f=h=1/3 and the Schwarzschild curvature of the dimensionless null potential, while the
inner light ring remains a genuinely new stable-trapping structure.
The purpose of the present work is not merely to diagnose the geodesic properties of this metric, but
to construct explicit local action jets capable of supporting the same background while satisfying
the no-ghost and Laplacian-stability conditions of scalar–tensor and genuine U(1) gauge-invariant
scalar–vector–tensor theories. The construction is divided into three dynamically distinct but over-
lapping radial domains. The weak-to-intermediate exterior is carried by a finite Horndeski G_3-G_4
sector. The central strong-field light-ring region is realized by a genuine scalar–vector–tensor inter-
action with background-integrable action jets. The regular inner region is supported by a quartic
Horndeski core. These are not left as unrelated effective descriptions: using a scalar-coordinate
C^\infty partition, background re-solving, transverse action-jet controls and the common unre-
duced even-parity constraint system, we construct a single smooth local same-action continuation
through both overlap bands.

1. Introduction
1.1 Geometry is not yet dynamics
A spacetime metric may possess attractive geometrical properties without corresponding to a dy-
namically viable theory.
This distinction becomes particularly important whenever a proposed strong-field geometry departs
from the standard Schwarzschild or Kerr solutions of general relativity. It is comparatively straight-
forward to prescribe metric functions that are asymptotically acceptable, regular near the center,
reproduce familiar weak-field behavior, or generate interesting null-geodesic structures. It is sub-
stantially harder to demonstrate that the same geometry can arise from a covariant field theory
whose physical perturbations carry positive kinetic energy, whose characteristic speeds are real,
and whose constraint structure does not become singular precisely in the region where the geometry
becomes phenomenologically interesting.
The present work addresses this second question for the P5 geometry developed within the Seg-

1.5 The central methodological diﬀiculty
The main technical problem is not simply finding enough free functions.

                                                   15
There are many free functions.
The real diﬀiculty is satisfying several logically distinct requirements simultaneously.
The target metric must solve the background equations.
The scalar profile must solve its own equation rather than merely the metric equations.
The vector background must remain regular.
The action jets used to repair perturbative instabilities must correspond to derivatives of a consistent
local action.
The kinetic matrices must remain nonsingular.
The physical eigenmodes must have positive kinetic energy.
Their squared radial and angular propagation speeds must remain positive.
The construction must remain finite across both light rings.
And finally, independently constructed Horndeski and genuine-SVT regions must be capable of
belonging to one common action.
An arbitrary pointwise assignment of perturbative coeﬀicients would not solve this problem.
The strategy developed here is therefore based on background-integrable action jets and
background-null perturbative controls.
The former ensure that the action derivatives required by the background equations arise from a
consistent local functional structure.
The latter use directions in higher action-derivative space that do not change the already-fixed
background solution but do change the quadratic perturbation operator.
This separation is what makes the construction tractable.
